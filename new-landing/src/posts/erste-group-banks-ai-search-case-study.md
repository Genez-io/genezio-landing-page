In April 2026, BCR was recommended in 921 of the 1,845 AI conversations Genezio ran across its six core product journeys. A recommendation rate of 49.9%, statistically indistinguishable from Revolut at 49.5%.

By August, BCR was recommended in 1,605 of 1,898 conversations: **84.6%, ±1.4 at a 90% confidence level**, with the nearest competitor 19 points behind.

![BCR and Erste Bank Hungary AI recommendation rate case study](/images/bcr-customer-success-story.webp)

Same bank, same product set, same competitors, five months apart. Here is what changed, and what the same method produced in a different market at Erste Bank Hungary, where the starting position was considerably worse.

## Why the number that moved was recommendation, not visibility

BCR was never invisible. Since tracking started in October 2025, the bank has been mentioned 71,539 times across 102,004 AI conversations. Presence was never the constraint, which is exactly what makes it a misleading metric.

The question that decides anything is different. When someone asks ChatGPT which bank to use for a mortgage refinance, does the model put BCR on the shortlist, or does it just name-check the market?

Those are separate measurements, and they diverge. Models named BCR freely in April and still recommended it in only half the conversations, level with a neobank that has a fraction of the branch network, the product depth and the balance sheet. The gap between "AI knows you exist" and "AI tells people to choose you" was the entire problem, and no visibility dashboard would have shown it. We have written before about why [visibility is only half the picture](/blog/visibility-is-half-the-picture/); BCR is the version of that argument with a P&L attached.

## The recommendation rate, month by month

Six Recommender topics (current account, mortgage, personal loan, credit card, mobile banking, savings and deposits) across 14 tracked brands.

| Month | BCR | Runner-up | BCR share of voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| April 2026 | 49.9% | Revolut 49.5% | 17.0% |
| May 2026 | 52.3% | Revolut 49.5% | 17.6% |
| June 2026 | 66.3% | ING 55.1% | 19.6% |
| July 2026 | 80.7% | ING 66.0% | 22.5% |
| August 2026 | **84.6%** | ING 65.9% | 23.3% |

Thirty-four and a half points in five months. Share of voice went from 17.0% to 23.3% in a market where 14 brands compete for the same answer space.

The topic-level picture in the last week of August: credit card 100%, mortgage 96.4%, personal loan 90.5%, current account 86.9%, mobile banking 84.5%, savings 71.4%. On the comparison-style topics, where users explicitly weigh BCR against a named rival, share of voice runs between 39% and 50%.

Sentiment across those topics in August: 66% positive, 24% neutral, 10% negative, out of 17,679 individual statements.

## What the setup looked like before it worked

BCR started tracking in October 2025 with six topics and 185 scenarios, built the way most teams build them at first, as questions written close to keywords. That architecture is still in the account, archived.

In March 2026 the structure changed. Every product area was split into three agents running different jobs:

- A **Recommender** agent asking the question a customer actually asks, with no brand named, to see who the model puts forward.
- A **Comparer** agent putting BCR head to head against a specific competitor.
- An **Introspector** agent asking what the model believes about BCR itself.

Then it got narrower rather than broader. In late March the business segment came in as its own tracked entity. In June, three student personas (Tudor, Maria, Andrei) each with their own financial situation and their own way of asking. In July, four topics dedicated to what Google's AI Overviews return.

The personas produced the sharpest reading. Andrei's scenarios put BCR at 85.7%. Maria's at 62.5%. Tudor's at 57.1%, with Revolut between 85% and 95% across all three.

One product, one market, one month, three customers, three different answers. A single prompt about student accounts would have averaged that into one number nobody could act on.

## The gaps were specific, and that is why they were fixable

Eighty-one insights have been delivered on the BCR account, generated daily. The useful ones never say "improve your content." They name a scenario and a number.

One from late August: BCR appears in 4% of conversations about interest-free credit card installments. Banca Transilvania appears in 96%. In the same topic, BCR reaches 93% on "credit card online." The bank is not missing from the card category. It is missing from the one benefit-specific question customers use to make the actual choice.

Another: across the four priority journeys, competitor-owned product pages picked up 584 citations in 30 days. ING's refinancing page, Raiffeisen's personal loan page, CEC's online account page. Those pages were the evidence AI used to answer questions about BCR's own market.

A third, sharper still: on the mortgage refinancing scenario and the "which bank has the lowest personal loan rate" scenario, comparison sites produced 29 and 20 citation occurrences respectively, and BCR produced zero first-party citations. Finzoom, Conso, Bankata and Finradar had become the proof layer for questions about BCR's products.

That is a content brief, not a finding. It names which page to write, for which query, against which competitor.

The citation ledger now reads differently. In the last week of August, across 696 distinct cited domains, **bcr.ro was the single most-cited domain in the market**, with 3,079 occurrences, ahead of revolut.com at 1,521 and ing.ro at 1,091.

## Erste Bank Hungary: a harder starting position

The Hungarian market is not the Romanian one. OTP Bank is an incumbent with a scale advantage no competitor in Romania holds, and the comparison sites (Bankmonitor, BiztosDöntés, Bank Navigator) carry real weight in how models answer.

Erste Bank Hungary started tracking in November 2025 with three topics. The first month's reading was blunt: **44.1% presence, fifth place**, behind OTP at 59.0%, K&H at 56.1% and CIB at 50.1%.

| Month | Erste Bank Hungary | OTP Bank | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| November 2025 | 44.1% | 59.0% | 5th |
| December 2025 | 70.6% | 64.1% | **1st** |
| February 2026 | 65.8% | 72.7% | 2nd |
| May 2026 | 72.1% | 72.2% | level |
| June 2026 | **80.8%** | 80.5% | **1st** |
| August 2026 | 69.8% | 71.7% | 2nd |

A 15-point deficit against the largest bank in the country became a coin flip. In August, 3,920 conversations were tracked in that market and Erste was present in 69.8% of them, 1.9 points behind OTP.

The build followed the same shape as BCR's, compressed. Three Prompter topics in November. Thirteen by mid-January, covering refinancing, the Munkáshitel salary-based loan, the Otthon Start first-home programme, student accounts, children's accounts, business accounts and the Babaváró family loan.

In May, a bank-switching Comparer built on the question Hungarian customers actually type: OTP versus Erste. Nineteen Recommender topics eleven days later.

**erstebank.hu is now the most-cited domain in the Hungarian banking answer space**: 13,400 occurrences in a single week across 1,048 distinct domains, ahead of otpbank.hu at 10,955.

## The part of the Hungarian data that is not a win

Presence and recommendation are moving in different directions at Erste, and the honest reading is more useful than the flattering one.

Since the Recommender topics went live in late May, Erste's recommendation rate has run 56.1% in June, 53.1% in July, 49.1% in August. OTP has stayed 3 to 6 points ahead the whole way. Sentiment sits at 50% positive against BCR's 66%, out of 35,339 statements.

Erste has won the visibility argument and has not yet won the recommendation one. The 83 insights on the account are mostly about that distance. The most recent names business account opening: Erste at 32% recommendation share against Gránit Bank at 71% and Raiffeisen at 63%, in a topic where demand is already proven.

The query "site:erstebank.hu vállalkozói számla online számlanyitás" appeared 30 times in a month, tied with OTP's equivalent. Gránit's account page was cited 394 times, surfacing on "fully online business account opening." Erste has the product. It does not yet have the page a model can quote.

That is a specific, buildable answer to a specific, measured loss, and it is the reason the Hungarian programme is worth writing about mid-flight rather than after the fact.

## What transfers between the two

Two banks in the same group, two markets, two very different starting positions, and the same three decisions behind the numbers.

They measured recommendation instead of presence, which is the only reason BCR's April problem was visible at all. They ran multi-turn conversations as configured personas rather than prompts, which is why one student segment could read 57% and another 86% in the same month for the same product. And they treated [confidence intervals](/blog/confidence-intervals-for-cmos/) as a requirement rather than a nicety: 84.6% ±1.4 survives a conversation with a CFO in a way that "we seem to be doing better" does not.

Across both accounts, that is 140,584 tracked conversations and 164 delivered insights, each one naming a scenario, a competitor and a gap.

The next front for both banks is narrower than the one they just cleared: the benefit-level questions asked by a customer who has already decided to buy and is choosing between two names. Interest-free installments in Romania. Online business account opening in Hungary.

Each is a single scenario worth a single page, and each is currently being answered by somebody else's page. That is where the next 10 points of recommendation rate sit, in both markets.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between AI visibility and AI recommendation rate?**
Visibility measures how often a brand appears anywhere in an AI response. Recommendation rate measures how often the model actually puts the brand forward as an option to choose. BCR's data shows why the distinction matters: presence looked healthy in April while recommendation sat level with a neobank. The two metrics moved independently. The full comparison is in [AI recommendation vs AI visibility](/blog/ai-recommendation-vs-ai-visibility/).

**How many conversations do you need before the numbers mean anything?**
Enough that the confidence interval is narrow enough to act on. BCR's August recommendation rate of 84.6% comes from 1,898 conversations that month across six topics, giving a 90% confidence interval of 83.2% to 85.9%. A few hundred prompts would have produced a range too wide to distinguish 50% from 85%.

**Why track personas instead of queries?**
Because the same product question produces different answers depending on who is asking. Across BCR's three student personas in the same month, recommendation rates were 57.1%, 62.5% and 85.7%. Averaging those into one query-level number hides both the loss and the win.

**Can AI search performance be improved in a specific market like Hungary or Romania?**
Yes, and local competitors and local comparison sites are the reason it has to be measured locally. In Hungary, Bankmonitor and BiztosDöntés shape answers in a way no Romanian equivalent does. In Romania, Finzoom and Conso occupy the proof layer on borrowing questions. The two markets required different content responses.
