![Track your brand from the AI Overview to AI Mode’s final recommendation](/images/google-ai-overview-ai-mode-follow-ups.webp)

**Showing up in Google's AI Overview doesn't mean you win the customer.** Most brands track whether they appear in that first AI answer at the top of the page. But that is where the search starts, not where the buyer decides.

The choice happens a question or two later, when the buyer asks Google to narrow it down. Until now, almost nobody was measuring that part.

Genezio now does. We follow the buyer from the AI Overview into the follow-up questions they ask in Google's AI Mode, and show you whether your brand is still there when it counts.

## The problem: the decision happens on the next question

Picture a marketing manager looking for a project management tool. She types "best project management software for agencies" into Google. An AI Overview at the top lists five options.

She doesn't stop there. She clicks "Ask anything" under the Overview and keeps going: "Which of these works best for a team of 10?" Then: "Which one should I pick?" Google remembers what it already told her and answers each question with that in mind. Two or three questions later, she has a favourite.

Most AI visibility tools only see pieces of this. They record the first answer on its own. Or they ask Google's AI Mode a question in a brand-new chat that knows nothing about the Overview. Neither is what the buyer actually saw.

That matters because the brands change along the way. A brand can be listed in the Overview and vanish when the buyer asks for the best fit. Another can be missing from the first answer and end up as the final recommendation.

![A Google search that starts in the AI Overview recommending Brand A and ends in AI Mode recommending Brand C after the buyer adds an SAP requirement and asks about price](/images/google-ai-mode-follow-up-conversation.webp)

Our data shows how often it happens. By the third question, about **80% of brands** are recommended less than they were in the AI Overview. A typical brand keeps only **69%** of its first-answer recommendations, and even the strongest keep under 80%. When a brand drops out, it rarely leaves an empty seat. In **80%** of lost recommendations, AI Mode names a competitor instead. Most losses (**about 60%**) happen when the buyer adds a new requirement, like a team size, a feature or a use case, that the brand's content doesn't clearly answer. It works the other way too. In about **1 in 4** conversations where a brand misses the first answer, it gets recommended later.

If you only measure the first answer, your report can say you're winning while you lose the decision, or the other way round. For a marketing team, that means reporting the wrong number to leadership and fixing the wrong content.

## The solution: follow the buyer through the whole conversation

Genezio now measures Google the way people use it:

1.  The first question is answered by the AI Overview, on a normal Google results page.
2.  Follow-up questions continue in AI Mode, opened from that same Overview through its "Ask anything" box, in the same session.

AI Mode already knows what the Overview said, just as it does for a real buyer. So you see how Google talks about your brand deeper in the conversation: when the user narrows the choice, compares options, or asks "which one should I pick?"

## What shows up in the dashboard

**One engine: "Google AI Overview + AI Mode".** On plans with this feature, AI Overview and its AI Mode follow-ups appear together as a single engine, because that is how users experience them.

**A label on every answer.** Each answer says where it came from, AI Overview or AI Mode. A divider marks the exact point where the conversation moved from one to the other.

**Sources credited to the right place.** If AI Mode links to a website in a follow-up, that counts as an AI Mode citation, not an AI Overview one. Your visibility and source reports stay accurate for each, so you can still compare them side by side.

**Global, with real customer personas.** The journey runs worldwide, on the local Google version of each region you track. In every market, Genezio asks the questions as your customer personas would, in their language, so you see what your actual buyers see.

## Measure the conversation, not the snapshot

Buying decisions on Google now happen across several AI turns, not on one results page. The Overview sets the shortlist. The follow-ups pick from it.

That is the same reason Genezio simulates full multi-turn conversations as customer personas on every engine, instead of firing single prompts. Google was the last place where the two surfaces were measured as unrelated snapshots. Now they are one journey.

The AI Overview gets you on the shortlist. The follow-up decides who gets picked, and 80% of the time you lose that spot, it goes to a competitor.
